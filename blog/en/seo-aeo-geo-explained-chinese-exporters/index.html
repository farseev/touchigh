SEO vs. AEO vs. GEO: What's the Difference, and Do Exporters Need All Three?

SEO gets you found, AEO gets your answer quoted by AI, and GEO gets you recommended by generative AI. They share one foundation, so exporters can run all three with one workflow.

What's the difference between SEO, AEO and GEO, and does an export business need all three? In short: SEO gets you found, AEO gets your answer quoted directly by AI, and GEO gets you recommended inside generative AI answers. They aren't three separate jobs. They're three outlets for the same content capability, and the easiest path is to run all three through one workflow.

The question has come up a lot lately. In August, Cifnews, a Chinese cross-border e-commerce news site, ran a contributor article arguing that as traffic gets more expensive, independent DTC sites should treat SEO plus GEO as their "ballast," building "SEO and GEO as assets, not burning them as expenses." On October 15, Search Engine Land reported that Google has adjusted AI Overviews and AI Mode so inline source links show up higher and more often, to make sure key sources are easy to click. AI answers and traditional search are growing into each other.

What are SEO, AEO and GEO?

SEO (search engine optimization) gets your pages ranked high in Google's and Bing's link results. Say an independent site selling portable power stations wants to reach page one for "portable power station for camping."

AEO (answer engine optimization) gets your content used directly as the answer. A buyer asks "what size power station do I need for camping," and Google's AI Overview or a voice assistant quotes the paragraph on your page.

GEO (generative engine optimization) gets your brand mentioned, cited and recommended in generative answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode. Say an overseas buyer asks ChatGPT "which power station OEM factories in China are reliable," and your name shows up in the answer.

The lines aren't absolute. Google's AI Overviews are a battleground for both AEO and GEO, and many of the pages AI cites are pages that already do well in SEO.

Why can't you do just one?

SEO alone: you may rank on page one, but buyers read the AI Overview and leave without scrolling down. GEO alone: without indexed, well-structured pages, AI has nothing to cite. AEO alone: you write plenty of Q&As, but without first-hand data and credible brand information behind them, AI may not bother quoting you.

Google's latest change shows the three merging. Per Search Engine Land, when a piece of information is available from only a few official websites, the AI answer lists those sources separately at the top. Google also says use of its Preferred Sources feature has grown 7x this year. Official, trusted sources that users choose are winning search rankings and AI citations at the same time. The report also notes, though, that Google hasn't yet released click data for these AI features in Search Console, so how many clicks actually flow back remains to be seen.

As for the Cifnews article, the author's line "with ads you look for customers; with SEO, customers look for you" points in a useful direction. But figures in it, such as "organic search is 40% to 60% of independent-site traffic" and "SEO traffic converts at 2.8 times the rate of paid traffic," come from third parties with no original source cited. Don't base budget decisions on them.

If products all look the same, why would AI recommend you?

Market structure theory in economics sorts markets into a few types. The more identical the products and the more sellers there are, the closer a market gets to "perfect competition," where every seller simply takes the market price. Only by differentiating can you move into "monopolistic competition" and win a little pricing power of your own. Many Chinese exporters on Alibaba.com (Alibaba's international B2B marketplace) and Amazon are stuck in the first situation: dozens of sellers offer the same product, and it all comes down to price.

The same thing is happening in search results and AI answers. If your English pages say "high quality, competitive price" like everyone else's, search engines and AI have no reason to pick you. Amid the echoes, authenticity is the lighthouse: only the test data, process details, real cases and founder views that you alone can provide will lift you out of the sameness.

An example: two stores sell pet water fountains. Store A writes "ultra-quiet." Store B states how many decibels the pump measured and under what conditions, with a test video and the method. To buyers, Store B is more credible. To search engines and AI, Store B offers a fact they can cite; Store A offers an adjective.

How do you run all three as one?

  1. List buyer questions. Sort them by buying stage: what buyers ask when learning about the category, when comparing suppliers and right before ordering. Inquiry logs, customer service records and trade show notes are all good sources.
  2. Write one page per question. Answer in the first two or three sentences for AEO; expand with details, comparisons and steps for SEO; add first-hand data, test results and sources for GEO.
  3. Get the technical basics right. Make sure pages are indexed, load fast on mobile and have clear titles and descriptions, and add structured data to product, FAQ and company information.
  4. Keep off-site information consistent. Use the same English brand name, main product categories and contact details on your independent site, Alibaba.com, Amazon, LinkedIn and YouTube, so AI recognizes them as one brand.
  5. Track all three kinds of metrics together. Each month, log rankings and organic traffic, appearances in featured snippets and AI Overviews, and whether you get cited when you ask ChatGPT and Perplexity a fixed set of questions.
  6. Review every quarter. Use your most-cited pages as templates and fix the weak ones, rather than just publishing more.

Here's a factory example. A maker of stainless steel insulated tumblers could write a page on "what affects how long an insulated tumbler keeps drinks hot." The first three sentences give the conclusion: the vacuum-layer process, the lid seal and the liner material matter most. The body shows heat-retention data from the factory's own lab under identical conditions, plus the test method. The bottom of the page lists certifications and production capacity. That one page has a shot at ranking in Google, being excerpted in an AI Overview and being cited by ChatGPT. That's "all three as one" in its simplest form.

Small team, tight budget: where do you start?

If you run an independent DTC site, lock down the SEO basics first: indexing, speed, and clear core product and category pages. Then add Q&A sections to your highest-traffic pages, which covers AEO along the way. If you're a B2B factory, start with GEO's "entity consistency" and "capability pages," because when buyers use AI to build a supplier shortlist, the first things they check are who you are and what you can make.

Frequently asked questions

Are AEO and GEO the same thing?

They overlap, but the focus differs. AEO is about whether your content can be used directly as an answer, such as a featured snippet or a quoted passage in an AI Overview, so it centers on question-and-answer writing. GEO is about whether your brand gets mentioned and recommended in generative answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity and others, which also depends on first-hand data and consistent brand information across the web. In practice, you can do both together.

Will GEO replace SEO?

Not anytime soon. When generative AI answers questions using the web, much of its information still comes from pages indexed by search engines, and Google is making source links in AI answers more visible. SEO is the foundation that gets your content found and indexed; GEO builds on it to earn citations and recommendations. Treating them as opposites usually means neglecting one of them.

Do AEO and GEO require technical changes to my website?

Usually not many. What matters most is the content itself: clearly stated questions, answers that lead with the conclusion, and data with sources. On the technical side, confirm pages can be crawled and indexed and load properly on mobile, then add structured data to your FAQ, product and company information. Most website builders handle this through plugins or settings.

We already sell on Alibaba.com and Amazon. Do we still need this?

Marketplace stores bring traffic and inquiries, but the platform sets the page rules, and your brand easily gets buried among similar products. Your independent site and official brand website are information sources you fully control, and an important reference when search engines and AI verify a brand. Keep selling on the platforms, and keep your website information consistent with them.
