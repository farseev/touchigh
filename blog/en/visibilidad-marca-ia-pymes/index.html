AI brand visibility: is ChatGPT recommending your small business?

AI assistants cite third-party sites far more than your own website. Here's how to check in one afternoon whether ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google mention your business, and what to fix first.

The question behind this article

How do I know if ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google recommend my business when a customer asks about what I sell?

Short answerTest it yourself: type 15 to 20 real buying questions from your industry into ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google (AI Overviews or AI Mode), and note whether you appear, in what position, in what tone and which sites get cited. Repeat the same list every month. If you don't show up, work first on the third-party sources the AI already consults.

Key takeaways

  • AI brand visibility measures whether assistants like ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini name or recommend you when a shopper asks about your category.
  • According to the Mencoro study reported by PuroMarketing, only 4.8% of AI citations in commercial queries in Spain point to the queried brand's own domain.
  • A fixed list of 15 to 20 questions, checked every month on the same engines, is enough to build a reliable baseline without paying for tools.
  • If AI doesn't mention you, your first front isn't your blog but the directories, marketplaces and industry sites it already reads.
  • Five simple metrics (mention, position, tone, sources and share versus competitors) fit in one spreadsheet and cover the essentials.

How do you know whether ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google recommend your business? Ask them what your customers ask, always with the same list, and write down who shows up and which sources get cited. You don't need a suite of tools to start. You need a method and a spreadsheet.

The topic is on the agenda because PuroMarketing published a guide to measuring it on October 14, with an uncomfortable number: according to a Mencoro study of more than 176,000 citations in AI answers to commercial queries in Spain, only 4.8% point to the brand's own domain. Another 72.6% go to other companies' websites in the same industry, such as competitors, distributors or retailers.

What is AI brand visibility?

It's how often an AI assistant mentions or recommends your company when someone asks a question about your category. Having a listing somewhere isn't enough. What matters is that the answer names you.

The difference from classic search runs deep. Google used to show ten links and the user picked one. Now the AI writes an answer, decides whom to name, and often the customer never clicks on anything else. You can rank well on Google and still be missing from that answer.

The work of improving that presence is called GEO (Generative Engine Optimization). It shares foundations with SEO (search engine optimization), but it chases something different: getting cited, not just getting found.

Where does AI get what it says about your business?

This is the point that changes a small business's strategy the most. The Mencoro study published by PuroMarketing breaks citations down like this:

  • 72.6%: websites of companies in the same industry (competitors, distributors, retailers).
  • 11.6%: business directories and marketplaces.
  • 4.8%: the brand's own domain.
  • 4.3%: news outlets and magazines.

Put another way, what others publish about you weighs far more than your own website. Each engine also has its own preferences. According to the same piece, Google gives special weight to social media, especially Instagram, while ChatGPT tends to favor institutional sources and press coverage.

Read these figures with the caution any vendor study deserves: the article doesn't spell out the time period or how the queries were chosen. Even so, the direction is clear, and you'll most likely see it confirmed in your own tests.

How to check in one afternoon whether AI recommends your business

  1. Write 15 to 20 real buying questions. Mix stages: “which is better, X or Y,” “best [service] in [city],” “affordable [service] for small businesses,” “[your brand] reviews.” Pull them from the questions customers send you on WhatsApp, by email or ask at the counter.
  2. Separate questions with and without your brand name. Generic ones measure whether you get recommended; branded ones measure what AI says about you.
  3. Run them on four engines. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google, either in its AI summaries (AI Overviews) or in AI Mode. Use a session with no history so your past searches don't color the answer.
  4. Record five data points per answer in a sheet laid out like the table below, along with the date, engine, language and country.
  5. Save the cited sources. Copy the URLs that appear in each answer. That's your to-do list.
  6. Repeat the same list every month. Models change and their answers vary. A single snapshot tells you nothing; the trend does.

A hypothetical example: a language school in Monterrey, Mexico, asks “best English classes for adults in Monterrey.” If the answer names three competitors and cites a local directory where the school doesn't even have a listing, it knows exactly where to start.

Five metrics that fit in a spreadsheet

PuroMarketing proposes five indicators. For a small business they're enough, and you can track them by hand:

MetricWhat you recordHow to read it
Mention rateYes or no for each questionOut of 20 questions, how many do you appear in?
Position in the answerBeginning, middle or endShowing up in the first block counts for more than a mention at the bottom
SentimentPositive, neutral or criticalA critical tone usually comes from real reviews or news
Cited sourcesThe URL of each sourceTells you which sites to look after, complete or correct
Share vs. competitorsWhich rivals appear and how oftenAccording to the article, the biggest ad spender doesn't always win

Why is the problem so hard to see with so many tools around?

If you run a business, you already live with too many open tabs: website analytics, Meta Business Suite, your store dashboard, email, reviews. Adding “AI visibility” sounds like one more dashboard, which is why so many owners put it off.

Psychology explains this with cognitive load theory: working memory can only juggle a few things at once, and when you flood it with scattered data, you stop seeing what matters. It isn't a lack of interest. It's too much noise.

The way out isn't another tool. It's simplifying: a fixed list of questions, five columns and a monthly date on your calendar. When everything fits on one sheet, the pattern shows itself: which questions you vanish from and which sources keep coming up.

What do I do if ChatGPT or Google don't mention me?

Start where the AI is already looking, not where you wish it would look:

  • Industry directories and marketplaces. Make sure your details are complete and current on the ones that show up as sources in your tests.
  • Clear answers on your website. A plain-language FAQ page, with ballpark prices, service areas and how you differ from other options, keeps the engine from having to guess.
  • Structured data. Structured data markup (schema) helps AI identify your products, hours or services without ambiguity.
  • Social media built for each engine. If your customers mostly search on Google, put care into visual content, because according to PuroMarketing that engine especially values Instagram.
  • Industry sites that already get cited. Comparison pages, trade associations and local media: ask to be included, or ask them to correct outdated details.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I check whether AI mentions my brand?

Once a month is enough for most small businesses. What matters is repeating exactly the same list of questions, on the same engines and in sessions with no history, so the comparisons are fair. Model answers vary from day to day, so a single query proves nothing. What counts is the trend over several months.

Does ranking first on Google guarantee I'll show up in ChatGPT?

No. Strong search rankings help, but they don't ensure an AI assistant will name you in its answer. Each engine picks its own sources: according to PuroMarketing, Google gives a lot of weight to social networks like Instagram, while ChatGPT tends to lean on institutional sources and the press. That's why it pays to measure your presence on each engine separately.

Do I need to pay for a tool to measure my AI visibility?

Not to start. With a spreadsheet, 15 to 20 buying questions and one afternoon a month, you can log mentions, position, tone, sources and competitors. Paid tools make sense when you want to track hundreds of queries, several markets or languages, or when you don't have time to repeat the tests with discipline.

Why does AI recommend my competitors and not me?

Usually because there's more information about them in the sources the AI consults. The Mencoro study cited by PuroMarketing found that 72.6% of citations in commercial queries in Spain go to websites of companies in the same industry, and 11.6% to directories and marketplaces. If you aren't present and up to date there, it's hard to get named.
