<p>How do Chinese ecommerce sellers get US shoppers to find, trust and engage with their brand now that low prices and duty-free shipping aren't enough? By building a brand people can look up, and making sure search engines and AI tools describe it correctly. That's what SEO, AEO and GEO do, and all three reward the same thing: clear, specific, verifiable information you publish yourself.</p><p>Two shifts make this urgent. <a href="https://qz.com/de-minimis-exemption-end-shein-temu-prices-100626" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Quartz</a> reported this month that the end of the de minimis duty exemption has raised prices at Shein and Temu, with the cheapest items hit hardest. One Tampa shopper's two Temu dog harnesses cost about $33, but import charges added roughly $46, bringing the total to "80-something dollars." Meanwhile, <a href="https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-jump-branded-queries-september-492962" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Search Engine Land</a> reported DemandSphere data showing Google's AI Overviews appeared on more than 80% of tracked branded queries by late September, up from about 26% at the start of the month. When shoppers search your brand name, Google's AI increasingly answers first.</p><h2 id="why-can-t-chinese-sellers-win-on-price-alone-anymore">Why can't Chinese sellers win on price alone anymore?</h2><p>For years the playbook was simple: list on a marketplace, price below everyone, and ship small parcels duty-free. De minimis made that work, because low-value packages skipped import duties. With that break gone, the price gap that carried many listings has shrunk, and the Quartz example shows how fast a bargain turns into an unpleasant surprise at the door.</p><p>When price stops being the reason to buy, shoppers need a different one. They recognize your name, they trust your quality, or you answered their question better than anyone else did. All three depend on being findable and credible outside the marketplace search box.</p><h2 id="what-are-seo-aeo-and-geo-in-plain-english">What are SEO, AEO and GEO, in plain English?</h2><p>Think of them as three layers of being found:</p><ul><li><strong>SEO (search engine optimization)</strong> is the classic work of getting your pages to rank in Google's results so people click through.</li><li><strong>AEO (answer engine optimization)</strong> is shaping content so search engines and voice assistants can lift it as the direct answer, often without a click.</li><li><strong>GEO (generative engine optimization)</strong> is getting AI tools such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode to mention and cite your brand when they generate an answer.</li></ul><div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Layer</th><th>Goal</th><th>Where it shows up</th><th>First move for a factory-turned-brand</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>SEO</td><td>Rank and earn the click</td><td>Google's regular results</td><td>One strong, well-structured page per product category</td></tr><tr><td>AEO</td><td>Be the quoted answer</td><td>Featured snippets, AI Overviews, voice assistants</td><td>An FAQ on shipping times, duties and returns, answer first</td></tr><tr><td>GEO</td><td>Be named and cited by AI</td><td>ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Mode</td><td>A clear About page and matching facts on every profile</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p>The layers stack. Good SEO makes your pages easy to find and read, AEO turns them into ready-made answers, and GEO is about whether AI tools trust those answers enough to name you.</p><h2 id="what-happens-when-a-shopper-googles-your-brand-now">What happens when a shopper Googles your brand now?</h2><p>Until recently, a brand-name search returned a list of links, and your own site usually sat near the top. According to Search Engine Land, AI Overview presence on tracked branded queries peaked at 90.48% on September 27, and Nectiv found AI Overviews for 93 of 100 enterprise brands it tested. The takeaway: brands now have to manage what Google's AI says about them, not just rank for their own name.</p><p>For a seller that grew up on marketplaces, this is a real risk. If your own site says little about who you are, the AI has to assemble an answer from whatever else it can find: marketplace listings, a forum thread, a reseller's page. If your site clearly states who makes your products, where, to what standards, and how shipping and returns work, the AI has better material to work with.</p><h2 id="why-self-reliance-beats-platform-dependency">Why self-reliance beats platform dependency</h2><p>Sociologists who study the <strong>anti-globalization movement</strong> describe a recurring pattern: when open, low-friction trade creates winners and losers, countries and communities push back with tariffs, new rules and a preference for local control. You don't have to take a side in that debate to see what it means for sellers. The end of de minimis is one expression of that pushback, and any business that depended on a single rule, platform or price advantage was exposed the moment it changed.</p><p>The answer isn't to quit marketplaces. It's to stop depending on them for everything. A brand people search for by name, a site you control, an email list you own and a social audience that knows your founder are assets no single policy change can wipe out overnight. True empowerment lies not in the freedom from dependency, but in the journey toward self-reliance.</p><h2 id="how-to-go-from-factory-listing-to-findable-brand-a-6-step-pl">How to go from factory listing to findable brand: a 6-step plan</h2><ol><li><strong>Audit what shoppers see today.</strong> Search your brand name plus "reviews," "legit" and "vs" on Google, then ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini about your category. Write down where you appear and what's said.</li><li><strong>Fix your About page.</strong> State the brand name, the company behind it, the factory location, years in production and certifications in plain text. Use the exact same name and description on your marketplace storefronts and social profiles.</li><li><strong>Answer the duty question before shoppers ask.</strong> After de minimis, "Will I pay import charges?" is a trust question. Say clearly on product pages and at checkout whether duties are included, and put shipping times and return terms in a short FAQ.</li><li><strong>Publish one genuinely useful guide per core category.</strong> Open with a two- or three-sentence answer, then add a comparison table and specs. One page like this serves SEO, AEO and GEO at once.</li><li><strong>Show the people and the process on social.</strong> Short TikTok, YouTube and Instagram videos of how products are made and tested prove that a real company stands behind the brand.</li><li><strong>Track your AI citation share monthly.</strong> Re-run the same questions and note whether you're cited and described accurately. ToucHigh's <a href="/#geo">GEO Autopilot</a> automates this: it finds where you're invisible across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, and Gemini, writes and ships the fixes, and tracks your AI-citation share over time.</li></ol><h2 id="where-does-audience-engagement-fit-in">Where does audience engagement fit in?</h2><p>Engagement is what happens after shoppers recognize you. A brand with a name, a face and honest answers gets comments, questions and repeat buyers. An anonymous listing gets price comparisons.</p><ul><li><strong>Turn comments into content.</strong> Every question that keeps coming up in your TikTok or Instagram comments is a candidate for your FAQ and your next guide.</li><li><strong>Reply like a maker, not a call center.</strong> A quick, specific answer from someone who knows the product builds more loyalty than a templated "Thanks for your support!"</li><li><strong>Move fans to channels you own.</strong> Offer something useful, such as a care guide or early access to new products, in exchange for an email address, so your best customers aren't at the mercy of an algorithm.</li></ul><p>Do these consistently and the layers reinforce each other: social conversations surface the questions, your site answers them, and search engines and AI tools pick up the answers.</p>

== faq ==
Q: Do I still need Amazon, Temu or TikTok Shop if I build my own site?
A: Usually, yes. Marketplaces still bring shoppers and sales, and dropping them overnight is risky. The goal is balance: use marketplaces for reach, and use your own site, search presence and email list to build a brand you control. Keep names and product facts consistent everywhere so shoppers and AI tools see one brand, not scattered listings.
Q: Does GEO replace SEO?
A: No. GEO builds on SEO. AI tools and Google's AI features still rely on pages that can be crawled, are well organized and are clearly written, so SEO basics remain the foundation. GEO adds a focus on direct answers, consistent facts about your brand across sources, and tracking whether AI tools actually cite you.
Q: How should I talk about import duties after de minimis?
A: Plainly and early. State on product pages and at checkout whether prices include duties and import fees, and explain any extra charges a shopper might see at delivery. Surprise fees damage trust fast, as the Quartz example of a roughly $33 order turning into about $80 shows. Clear answers also make strong FAQ content for search and AI.
Q: How long does it take for AI tools to start mentioning my brand?
A: There's no fixed timeline. It varies by AI tool, by category and by how much credible information about you already exists. Pick a set of shopper-style questions, ask them monthly in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Mode, and track whether you're mentioned and described accurately. Judge the trend, not a single result.
