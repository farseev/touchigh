<h2 id="how-should-cross-border-sellers-choose-sales-channels-the-sh">How should cross-border sellers choose sales channels? The short answer</h2><p>“How do I break into the US market and get American shoppers to actually accept my brand?” Once you get down to execution, the first decision is where to sell. The answer: <strong>pick your main channel based on your product and your own capabilities, then look at platform subsidies. One main channel plus one supporting channel makes it easier to stand out than listing everywhere.</strong></p><p>The question just got more urgent. A September 24 article from <a href="https://www.cifnews.com/article/189084" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Cifnews</a>, the Chinese cross-border trade outlet, says TikTok Shop US has started “tightening while it grows.” And according to a <a href="https://www.cifnews.com/article/189412" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Cifnews</a> report on October 13, SHEIN opened seller sign-ups on October 12 for its “Super Subsidy Double 11” and “Super Subsidy Black Friday” campaigns, with its year-end promotion starting October 19. (Double 11 is November 11, China’s Singles’ Day shopping festival.) One platform is getting stricter while the other is handing out more resources, and many Chinese sellers aren’t sure which way to bet.</p><h2 id="what-just-changed-at-tiktok-shop-and-shein">What just changed at TikTok Shop and SHEIN?</h2><h3>TikTok Shop: tightening on four fronts at once</h3><ul><li><strong>Compliance moves up front</strong>: Toys, children’s products and some home-safety items need a valid CPC (Children’s Product Certificate) or GCC (General Certificate of Conformity) plus test reports, and details such as model, brand, manufacturer and test date must match the listing. The article’s read is that review is getting closer to pre-approval.</li><li><strong>Fulfillment scoring</strong>: The platform cares less about whether you have a tracking number and more about whether the parcel actually entered the carrier’s network. Fake pickup scans and tracking that stalls for long stretches are risk signals.</li><li><strong>Limits on AI content</strong>: Using AI voiceovers, pre-recorded audio or static image slideshows in place of a real person selling live is treated as an account-health risk. The article’s rule of thumb: AI handles efficiency, people handle expression.</li><li><strong>Tougher competition</strong>: More look-alike products, pricier creators and more visible price wars.</li></ul><h3>SHEIN: subsidies plus traffic for the year-end push</h3><p>According to the report, approved products get both platform traffic and official subsidies; high-demand and trending items have their own submission channel; and sellers from China’s manufacturing clusters can join SHEIN’s semi-managed program with zero onboarding fees. The seller featured in the article runs a “small batch, fast reorder” model: stock 30 to 40 pairs per style first, and treat 15 to 20 pairs sold per week as the sign a style has potential. All of this comes from SHEIN’s recruitment messaging, so check the sign-up page for actual subsidy levels.</p><h2 id="why-does-a-busier-channel-make-it-harder-to-make-money">Why does a busier channel make it harder to make money?</h2><p>In economics, <strong>market structure theory</strong> sorts markets by how many sellers there are and how different their products are. The more sellers and the more alike their products, the closer a market gets to “perfect competition”: nobody has pricing power, everyone competes on price, and margins get squeezed thin. Flip it around: as long as shoppers see your product as different, you keep a little pricing room of your own.</p><p>That is exactly where many sellers in the US market find themselves. A hit product appears, and within weeks the platform fills up with copies. Subsidies can deliver a burst of sales, but they can’t make shoppers remember you. TikTok Shop limiting AI stand-ins for live selling is, at heart, the platform fighting sameness too: when everyone uses the same template, real people and real use cases become the scarce thing.</p><p>ToucHigh’s competitive-content research sees a similar pattern: about 87% of social content crowds into the same “authentic” formula. In a sea of echoes, content that is truly yours is the light that helps shoppers recognize you.</p><h2 id="which-major-channels-suit-which-sellers">Which major channels suit which sellers?</h2><div class="tw"><table><thead><tr><th>Channel</th><th>How it grows</th><th>Best for</th><th>Watch out for now</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>TikTok Shop US</td><td>Content and creators drive purchases</td><td>Products that need a demo; you can put a real person on camera</td><td>Up-front compliance documents, fulfillment scoring, AI content limits</td></tr><tr><td>SHEIN (incl. semi-managed)</td><td>Platform traffic plus subsidies, driven by styles</td><td>Many styles; a factory that can do small batches and fast reorders</td><td>Price and margin after subsidies end; stocking rhythm</td></tr><tr><td>Amazon</td><td>Driven by search intent, built on reviews</td><td>Standard products with a stable supply chain and after-sales support</td><td>Ad costs and identical-product competition</td></tr><tr><td>Independent DTC site</td><td>Owned traffic and repeat purchases that build a brand</td><td>Sellers who want their own customer data and a brand premium</td><td>Cold-start traffic; you need search, AI search and social to win customers</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p>“Best for” is a starting point, not a verdict. An independent DTC (direct-to-consumer) site is your own online store, outside any marketplace. And the same factory’s hero product and its new launches may belong on completely different channels.</p><h2 id="four-steps-to-choose-your-channel-mix">Four steps to choose your channel mix</h2><ol><li><strong>First, ask whether the product needs explaining.</strong> Products whose results need showing, like cleaning supplies, beauty tools and small appliances, depend more on content channels; standard products people understand at a glance fit search-driven channels better.</li><li><strong>Next, ask whether your supply chain can move fast.</strong> Only if you can test small batches and reorder quickly can you handle the traffic that promotional subsidies bring. If you can’t, the subsidy may bring you stockouts and bad reviews instead.</li><li><strong>Then work out your real take-home margin.</strong> Use the breakdown from the Cifnews article: account for product cost, logistics, creator commission, platform fees and after-sales cost to arrive at final profit. Run it once for the subsidy period and once for after.</li><li><strong>Finally, settle on one main channel and one supporting channel.</strong> Put most of your effort into the main one and use the supporting one to test products or clear inventory. Review every six months before deciding whether to add a third.</li></ol><p>If you sell several product lines, run these four steps for each product; the answers may differ.</p><p>A hypothetical example: a pet-supplies factory with many styles that can reorder within a week might start by using SHEIN’s promotions for volume and to find its bestsellers. Once those bestsellers are steady, it could move them onto TikTok Shop, with a designer or factory staff on camera explaining the workmanship and materials, slowly building brand recognition.</p><h2 id="what-should-you-check-this-week-before-the-holiday-promotion">What should you check this week before the holiday promotions?</h2><p>Whatever channel you choose, it’s worth spending one day before the year-end promotions checking your foundations. These items come from the Cifnews article’s advice, so just work through them:</p><ul><li><strong>Trace your compliance chain</strong>: Check item by item along product → supplier → test report → CPC/GCC → listing, and confirm that model, manufacturer, test date and age grading match all the way through.</li><li><strong>Spot-check tracking</strong>: Pull the last 30 days of orders and see how long it took from shipment to the first valid scan, and whether the first scan location makes sense.</li><li><strong>Prepare three kinds of content for each hero product</strong>: one pain-point video, one comparison and one use-case video, so you don’t rush to switch products when a single video doesn’t take off.</li><li><strong>Check how you use AI</strong>: Don’t loop pre-recorded audio in a livestream, and don’t pair product images with an AI voice to fake a live host. Use AI for scripts, captions and organizing footage.</li></ul><h2 id="if-you-change-channels-how-do-us-shoppers-still-remember-you">If you change channels, how do US shoppers still remember you?</h2><p>A channel is just a shelf. What shoppers remember is what you say and how you say it. Whatever platform you pick, these three things are worth starting now:</p><ul><li><strong>Put real people in your content</strong>: The factory owner, a designer or a quality inspector can all appear on camera. AI writes scripts, adds captions and organizes footage; people do the talking.</li><li><strong>Say one thing others can’t</strong>: Materials, craftsmanship, use cases. Find the one point copycat sellers can’t make, and repeat it.</li><li><strong>Leave clear information outside the platforms</strong>: AI assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity now answer questions like “which brand is good?” If there’s no clear description of your brand online, you don’t exist in those answers.</li></ul><p>Before you act, find out where you’re currently invisible. <a href="/#products">ToucHigh</a>’s day-one audit reads your store, plus your competitors’ ads and organic content, then flags where you’re missing from US AI search and where your social content falls short, so you can decide which channel deserves your effort next.</p>

== faq ==
Q: Is TikTok Shop US still worth it?
A: Yes, but the bar is rising. According to Cifnews, TikTok Shop's US GMV (gross merchandise value) still grew sharply in the first half of 2026, but compliance review is moving up front, fulfillment scoring is stricter, and replacing live hosts with AI is treated as a risk. It suits sellers who can complete their compliance paperwork and put real people on camera; there's less and less room for mass listing and pure-AI content.
Q: Which sellers are a good fit for SHEIN's subsidized promotions?
A: Sellers with many styles, their own factory and the ability to produce small batches and reorder fast. Subsidies and traffic can multiply sales quickly, but only if restocking keeps up. Before signing up, calculate your margin separately for the subsidy period and for after it ends, so you don't anchor your price at an unsustainable level just to chase volume.
Q: How many channels should one seller run at once?
A: For most small and midsize sellers, one main channel plus one supporting channel is safer: the main channel gets most of the effort and budget, and the supporting one is for testing products or clearing inventory. Every extra channel means another set of rules, another ad dashboard and another set of content requirements. Once the main channel is stable and the team has capacity, consider a third, such as an independent site.
Q: What are CPC and GCC certificates?
A: A CPC (Children's Product Certificate) applies to products designed mainly for children 12 and under; a GCC (General Certificate of Conformity) applies to other products covered by US consumer product safety rules. According to Cifnews, TikTok Shop US is moving certificate checks for toys, children's products and some home-safety items up front, and certificate details must match the listing.
