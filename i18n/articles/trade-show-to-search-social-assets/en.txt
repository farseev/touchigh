== q ==
We spend a lot every year on the Canton Fair and overseas trade shows, and inquiries dry up as soon as the show ends. How do we turn that exposure into a lasting source of leads?
== a ==
Treat each trade show as your annual "content-capture day," not just a business-card hunt. Before the show, announce it and book meetings. During it, record buyers' real questions, film process explainers and appear on camera with customers and partners. Follow up within 72 hours, then spend 12 months turning the footage into website articles, FAQs and LinkedIn and YouTube content, so buyers still find you when they search or ask AI.
== takeaways ==
- A trade show's costs land in a few days, and if you only count business cards, the payoff fades fast; turning the footage into content stretches the return across a full year.
- The questions buyers ask at your booth are the questions they'll later search on Google and ask AI, so write each one up as an FAQ or article.
- A trade show is the cheapest place to shoot video: products, venue, team and real buyer questions are all already there.
- Content created with customers, distributors and partners gets shared more often and earns mentions from third parties.
- Measure trade-show content by branded search, inquiry sources, whether you appear in AI answers, and the share of inquiries that come from content.

<p>You spend heavily on the Canton Fair and overseas trade shows every year, and inquiries dry up the moment the show ends. How do you turn that exposure into a lasting source of leads? The answer: <strong>treat each show as your annual "content-capture day."</strong> Business cards go stale, but the questions buyers asked at your booth, the process demos you gave on the spot and the conversations you recorded with customers and partners can all become content that Google and AI keep finding for a whole year.</p><p>The pace of going global is speeding up. Q: The organizer already has an exhibitor directory and online pages. Do we still need our own content?
A: Yes. Exhibitor directories use a standard format with limited space, so thousands of exhibitors look much the same, and the pages are rarely updated. Only your own website and social accounts can explain your processes, certifications and differences, and build up over time under your own domain and accounts. Use the directory as an entry point, but it can't replace your content assets.
Q: What should we watch out for when filming customers and partners at a show?
A: Get consent first, ideally in writing, stating which platforms the content will appear on. Keep their unreleased products, prices and order details out of frame. Agree on interview questions in advance and focus on industry issues and working together, not a hard sell. Ask them to approve before publishing and invite them to share afterward; that's how collaboration lasts.
Q: We don't have a professional video team. How do we make trade-show content?
A: A phone, a lavalier mic and a handheld stabilizer are basically enough. The value of trade-show content lies in real questions and explanations, not polished visuals. The key is dividing roles in advance: assign one person to film and log buyer questions full time, rather than having sales reps film while they greet visitors.
Q: How long should we keep publishing content from one show?
A: Plan for a full year. In the two weeks after the show, publish the recap and the most frequent questions; after that, pick one clip a week from your library to turn into new content, and write one in-depth article a month. By your next show, the landing page and related content will have built rankings and engagement, ready for the next round of pre-show promotion.
