== body ==
<p>Do social posts help you get cited by AI, and is Threads worth your time for SEO? For Google's AI answers, the evidence says social activity matters a lot. For ChatGPT, it matters far less. For Threads specifically, nobody has published clean numbers yet, so the smart move is to treat it as one public channel inside a system you own.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/ai-favors-brands-with-an-active-social-media-presence/832450/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Social Media Today</a> covered research from SE Ranking that looked at 100,000 keywords across many niches. Social platform links appeared in 67.66% of Google AI Overview responses and 32.39% of Google AI Mode responses, but in only 8.4% of ChatGPT answers. In AI Mode, social links showed up in 48.58% of non-branded queries, the searches where people haven't typed a brand name yet.</p>

<h2 id="what-does-ai-favors-active-social-brands-actually-mean">What does "AI favors active social brands" actually mean?</h2>
<p>It means AI answer engines, Google's above all, often link to social posts and profiles when they build an answer. If your brand has useful public posts on the topic someone asks about, you have more chances to be pulled into that answer. Making that happen on purpose is the core of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO): making your business easy for AI answer engines to find, trust and cite.</p>
<p>The details matter. According to the study, YouTube was the most cited source overall in AI Overviews. In AI Mode, Facebook, YouTube and Reddit were cited most. In ChatGPT, Reddit and LinkedIn were the top social platforms, though each still appeared in fewer than 1% of answers to common queries.</p>
<p>One honest caveat: the study counted how often social links appear. It doesn't prove that posting more makes a given brand appear. Social Media Today itself notes there's "no one-size-fits-all approach" and that optimizing for AI chatbots needs a platform-specific plan.</p>

<h2 id="does-threads-help-with-seo-and-ai-search">Does Threads help with SEO and AI search?</h2>
<p>Here is what we know and what we don't. The SE Ranking data covered by Social Media Today doesn't break out Threads at all. So any claim that Threads SEO is a shortcut to AI citations is, for now, a guess.</p>
<p>Threads is still worth testing for one plain reason: short, public, text answers to real questions are easy for search engines and AI tools to pick up and quote, and Threads is built for exactly that kind of post. Whether yours are actually being found is something you can check in five minutes. Search Google for your brand name plus "Threads" and see what comes up. Then ask Google AI Mode, Perplexity and ChatGPT a few questions your customers ask, and look at which sources they cite.</p>
<p>If your posts show up, keep going. If they never do, Threads can still be a good place to talk with customers, but don't count it as your AI visibility plan.</p>

<h2 id="why-shouldn-t-social-be-your-whole-ai-visibility-plan">Why shouldn't social be your whole AI visibility plan?</h2>
<p>Because platforms change the rules without asking you. Social Media Today, citing Search Engine Land, noted that Reddit's share of ChatGPT search citations fell 86.4% in four days in August. A business whose AI visibility lived entirely on Reddit would have watched it shrink in under a week through no fault of its own.</p>
<p>Sociologists who study the anti-globalization movement describe the same instinct on a much larger scale: communities that feel too dependent on distant systems they can't influence start rebuilding things they control locally. You don't need to share the politics to borrow the lesson. Depending on one platform for reach is a risk. Building something you own is the fix.</p>
<p>For a small business, the thing you own is your website: product pages, FAQs, guides and clear facts about who you are and what you sell. Social posts are the public echoes that make those facts easier to find and more likely to be repeated. The echo is useful. The hub is what lasts.</p>

<h2 id="how-do-i-build-a-social-to-ai-visibility-loop">How do I build a social-to-AI visibility loop?</h2>
<p>Run this every week. Once it's set up, it takes about two hours.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Collect 10 real questions.</strong> Pull them from customer emails, DMs, reviews and sales calls. Write them the way customers say them, such as "Is this rug safe for a kid's room?"</li>
<li><strong>Answer each one on your site first.</strong> Add a clear, short answer to an FAQ or guide page, with your brand name, product name and the specific facts.</li>
<li><strong>Post the short version on social.</strong> Turn each answer into a Threads post, a LinkedIn post or a short video. Lead with the question, answer it in the first two lines and link to the full page.</li>
<li><strong>Match the platform to the engine.</strong> If Google AI Overviews matter most to you, give YouTube priority. If you sell B2B and care about ChatGPT, LinkedIn deserves time. Keep Threads as a test channel until your own checks show it being picked up.</li>
<li><strong>Answer in public.</strong> When people ask questions in comments or replies, answer with substance. A public, specific reply helps the next person with the same question. "DM us" helps nobody else.</li>
<li><strong>Check citations monthly.</strong> Ask the same 10 questions in Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT and Perplexity. Note whether your site or your posts are cited. The share of answers that cite you is your AI-citation share.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="what-should-i-measure-instead-of-likes">What should I measure instead of likes?</h2>
<p>Likes tell you a post was pleasant. They don't tell you whether an AI tool will ever mention you. Add three numbers to your monthly report:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>AI-citation share:</strong> out of your 10 test questions, how many answers cite your site or your social posts.</li>
<li><strong>Non-branded appearances:</strong> how often you show up when the question doesn't include your brand name. That's where the 48.58% AI Mode figure suggests social activity counts most.</li>
<li><strong>Clicks to your hub:</strong> visits from social posts to the pages that hold your answers.</li>
</ul>

== faq ==
Q: How can I tell if my Threads posts show up in search?
A: Search Google for your brand name plus the word Threads, and for a few distinctive phrases from your best posts. Then ask Google AI Mode, Perplexity and ChatGPT the questions your posts answer and check the sources they cite. Repeat monthly. If your posts never appear, keep Threads for conversation and put your visibility effort into your site, YouTube or LinkedIn.
Q: Why do Google's AI answers cite social media more than ChatGPT does?
A: The study covered by Social Media Today shows the gap but doesn't fully explain it. The article connects Reddit's strong presence in Google's AI results to Google's commercial data-sharing deal with Reddit. More broadly, each answer engine draws on different sources, which is why optimizing for AI search has to be planned platform by platform rather than with one tactic.
Q: What is GEO and how is it different from SEO?
A: SEO (search engine optimization) helps your pages rank in a list of links. GEO, or Generative Engine Optimization, helps your business get mentioned and cited inside AI-written answers from tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. The two overlap, since clear, well-structured pages help both. GEO adds a focus on direct answers, consistent brand facts and presence on sources AI tools quote.
Q: Should I post the same thing on Threads, LinkedIn and Facebook?
A: Post the same answer, not the same words. Keep the facts identical so AI tools see a consistent picture of your business, but adapt the format: a short, direct reply on Threads, a fuller expert take on LinkedIn, a practical post or video on Facebook. Each version should link back to the page on your site that holds the full answer.
