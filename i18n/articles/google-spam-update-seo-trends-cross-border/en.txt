<p>Google has shipped another update, and new SEO (search engine optimization) buzzwords keep piling up. If you sell cross-border, which trends actually deserve your attention so competitors don't pull ahead? The short version: you don't have to chase them all. Read the September spam update and the AI Mode changes side by side, and only five things in 2026 need your hands on them. Everything else can go on a watch list.</p>

<p>Start with the news. According to <a href="https://www.seroundtable.com/google-september-2026-spam-update-done-42235.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Search Engine Roundtable</a>, Google's September 2026 spam update began around noon US Eastern time on September 24 and finished at 4:37 a.m. on October 8, roughly two weeks. This year's March, June and August spam updates took about one, two and three days. Google says this one is global and applies to all languages, and that affected sites may need several months to recover.</p>

<p>Almost at the same time, <a href="https://searchengineland.com/google-updates-ai-mode-and-ai-overview-with-more-links-context-carousels-and-information-on-creators-496242" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Search Engine Land</a> reported that Google is reworking AI Overviews (the AI summaries above the results) and AI Mode (its chat-style search). It is embedding more inline links in AI answers so they show up higher and more often, making sure key sources are easy to click.</p>

<h2 id="what-does-google-s-september-spam-update-mean-for-cross-bord">What does Google's September spam update mean for cross-border sellers?</h2>

<p>"All languages" means the English-language and multilingual sites that Chinese teams build for overseas buyers are fully in scope. Per Search Engine Roundtable's roundup, this update did not target link spam, and Google did not announce any policy changes alongside it.</p>

<p>Two weeks is not unusual historically. By the site's count, the August 2025 spam update ran 27 days, and the March 2024 and October 2023 updates ran 15 days each. This one stood out because it broke this year's pattern of wrapping up in a day or two, even though Google still describes it as a routine update.</p>

<p>Google's documentation is blunt: sites whose rankings change after an update should review their pages against its spam policies. For sellers, the first place to look is pages that were mass-produced, look alike and carry no real information. Think hundreds of landing pages where only the city or color name changes, or product descriptions copied word for word from a supplier's spec sheet.</p>

<p>Because recovery can take months, if your traffic dropped noticeably between September 24 and October 8, start cleaning up now instead of waiting it out.</p>

<h2 id="ai-mode-added-more-links-opportunity-or-gimmick">AI Mode added more links. Opportunity or gimmick?</h2>

<p>Beyond more inline links, Search Engine Land reported several other changes. When only a handful of official websites can provide information on something, a dedicated source block appears at the top of the AI answer and explains what those links are for. Article carousels for timely topics are expanding to more regions, languages and query types. Some answers now show thumbnails next to "related articles." And in some AI Mode video results, clicking a creator's name opens that creator's Google Search profile page. Google also says use of its Preferred Sources feature has grown sevenfold this year.</p>

<p>Still, in the same report Barry Schwartz is cautious about whether these changes will actually send clicks, and Google has not yet released click data for these links in the Search Console AI performance report. The sensible stance: work to become a linked source, but don't count on a flood of clicks right away.</p>

<p>For brand sellers, the official source block matters most. When shoppers ask about your product specs, warranty policy or after-sales process, Google leans toward showing the official website directly. If that information is patchy on your independent store (your own direct-to-consumer site, as opposed to a marketplace listing), a reseller or third-party site may take that spot.</p>

<h2 id="why-does-chasing-seo-trends-make-you-more-anxious">Why does chasing SEO trends make you more anxious?</h2>

<p>Cognitive load theory, from psychology, says working memory has limited room. Juggle too much information at once and nothing is left over for real understanding or action. Open ten SEO tools, subscribe to twenty newsletters, get a ranking-swing alert every day, and you usually end up knowing a little about everything while changing nothing.</p>

<p>So the best cure for trend anxiety is not reading more. It is subtraction: keep only what bears directly on your business and what you can act on this week. The table below was filtered by exactly that rule.</p>

<p>Number 4 is the one cross-border sellers overlook most. Google describes Preferred Sources as a way to help people choose the sources they want to see in Search. A feature like that rewards long-running, consistent, recognizable content sections, not one-off viral posts. If your blog only gets updated before big sales events, it's time to set a fixed cadence.</p>

<h2 id="an-seo-checkup-you-can-run-this-week">An SEO checkup you can run this week</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Check traffic against the timeline.</strong> In Google Search Console, look at impressions and clicks around September 24 to October 8 and flag pages with clear drops.</li>
<li><strong>Clean up low-quality pages.</strong> Merge, rewrite or take down thin and duplicate pages so they don't drag down the whole site.</li>
<li><strong>Write an "official answer page" for each core product.</strong> One page per hero product. Open with three sentences on what it is, who it's for and why it beats similar products, then add a spec table and an FAQ.</li>
<li><strong>Make your brand information consistent.</strong> Keep your English brand name, company description and after-sales policy identical across your independent site, Amazon and social accounts, so Google and AI tools can tell you are the official source.</li>
<li><strong>Add AI citation share to your report.</strong> Pick a fixed set of core questions and check every month whether Google AI Overviews, AI Mode and AI answer engines such as ChatGPT cite you.</li>
</ol>

<p>None of these five steps needs a new tool, and one marketer can finish the first round in a week. Once you have, your read on the "trends" shifts from hearsay to what you've seen in your own data.</p>

<p>Keep a watch list and check it once a month: new SEO tools, small daily ranking swings, rumors about some platform's next feature. Move an item to your action list only when it bears directly on your category and your buyers.</p>

== faq ==
Q: My site's traffic dropped in late September. Was it hit by the spam update?
A: Possibly, but confirm it first. Check whether the drop happened during the update window of September 24 to October 8, then whether it is concentrated on mass-produced, thin pages. If both match, follow Google's advice and review those pages one by one against its spam policies. Search Engine Roundtable reported that recovery may take months, so the sooner you clean up, the better.
Q: Will Google treat AI-written articles as spam?
A: What matters is not whether AI wrote it but whether it helps readers. The real risk is pages produced in bulk with no human review, pages that look alike and carry no real information. When you write with AI help, add your own product data, test results and real customer questions, and have a person check everything before it goes live.
Q: Will links in AI Mode send clicks to my site?
A: It's not clear yet. Google says inline links will appear higher and more often, but it has not released click data for these links in the Search Console AI performance report, and Search Engine Land's coverage is cautious about the click impact. Treat being cited as brand exposure, and keep measuring real return by organic orders.
Q: Do small sellers need to change their sites after every Google update?
A: No. As long as your content is genuine, useful and not mass-produced filler, most updates won't call for big changes. A practical rhythm: check your traffic curve after each major update finishes, run a full-site content checkup every quarter, and spend the rest of your energy on core product pages and buyer questions.
